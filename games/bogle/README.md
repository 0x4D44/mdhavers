# Bogle Castle, stage 5c

The engine is a pure integer portal renderer. Positions and heights in game state
use signed 16.16 integers; map coordinates are integer units. Angles cover one
turn in 4096 steps, with zero east and 1024 north. The committed sine table is
generated by `python3 games/bogle/tools/gen_trig.py`; projection needs no tangent
or arctangent table. Walls use perspective-correct texture columns and 16.16 vertical steps. Floors
and ceilings sample world texels per column. The fixed palette has 32 baked
light maps with sector and distance shading. Sprites face the player, sort far
to near, and clip against the screen and portal drawsegs; index 255 is transparent.
The tartan ghost and the cylindrical sky are full bright. Wall pictures use baked wall composites and follow wall perspective; only actors and free-standing dressing face the player.

Run from the repository root with `MDHAVERS_HOME` pointing there:

```sh
mdhavers run games/bogle/shell/headless.braw
mdhavers build games/bogle/shell/native.braw -o .scratch/bogle
.scratch/bogle
```

Startup shows the castle splash with **Press ony key**, then the title menu:
**New Game**, **Controls**, **Kit List**, **Quit**. Navigate menus with Up/Down or W/S and
choose with Enter/Space. Escape during play opens **Resume**, **Restart Level**,
**Controls**, **Kit List**, **Music ON/OFF**, **Quit**; Escape also resumes or returns from Controls or the Kit List. Menus
freeze the simulation and release the mouse. Resume captures it again. Held
menu keys must be released before they can fire or choose another item.

WASD moves, the mouse and Left/Right arrows turn, E uses doors and the exit,
Space or left mouse fires, and 1–4 select owned weapons. Hold fire for at
least 21 tics with the bagpipes, then release to blast; holding 35 tics fires
automatically. Controls are also available from the title and pause menus.
Each new level or restart shows its map-authored briefing; press any fresh key or mouse button to begin. The HUD objective advances from finding the airn key to finding the siller key, then reaching the gate. **M** toggles music on every screen.
Death offers **Restart** and **Back to Title**. Completing the Gatehouse offers
the same choices: there is no next level yet. Both screens show elapsed level
time from the existing 35 Hz tic count; kills are not tracked, and secrets are counted in game state only.

E1M1 starts in the courtyard facing a visible gamekeeper. Follow the courtyard
east to the gate; face the door within 64 units and **E: open** appears. Locked
doors name the missing iron or silver key. Moving gates have portcullis faces;
the iron-key gallery door uses the cell-door face, so the two keyed doors also
look different. Faces are assigned to both room-side portal walls when doors
are initialized, preserving explicitly authored wall textures. Use/key prompts
are centered above the crosshair and highest weapon tip at 320x200.
The gatehouse, hall, kitchen, crypt,
chapel and battlement exit remain reachable.

The shell exposes `bogle_init(w,h)` and `bogle_frame(dt_ms)` in
`shell/frame.braw`; the native host still owns the tiny loop. The bridge-free
`shell/frontend.braw` state machine accepts explicit held menu bits and gameplay
input, advances an exact 35 Hz accumulator with at most four tics per frame,
and draws each screen in the game font. Menu time and mouse motion are discarded
on transitions. Native windowed play is a manual acceptance check.

The native headless executable accepts `<map> <replay> <w> <h> <every>`. The
interpreter CLI accepts only a script filename, so both backends also accept
`BOGLE_MAP`, `BOGLE_REPLAY`, `BOGLE_W`, `BOGLE_H`, and `BOGLE_EVERY`. Defaults are
the test map, `replays/stage2.txt`, 80, 50, and 35. Each replay row contains a key
bitmask and mouse delta. Bits are forward 1, back 2, left strafe 4, right strafe
8, turn left 16, turn right 32, fire 64, use 128, and weapon selections
256/512/1024/2048. Positive mouse delta turns right. Each hash row
is a one-based tic followed by unsigned decimal FNV-1a of the indexed bytes.
The final tic is hashed even when it falls between sampling intervals.
`BOGLE_LIMIT` caps a replay prefix; `BOGLE_REQUIRE_EXIT=1` asserts completion
alive with both keys and all four weapons.

Native `--record games/bogle/replays/name.txt` or `BOGLE_RECORD` records inputs
actually consumed by tics. Mouse deltas accumulate between tics and are consumed
once, including frames with several due tics. Starting a new game or restarting
truncates the recording so it always contains one uninterrupted level attempt;
menu frames never enter the gameplay recording.

`tools/mapbuild.braw` provides `new_map`, `room`, `split_convex`, `door`, `stairs`,
`lift`, `secret_door`, `switch_wall`, `map_thing`, `validate`, and `write`. The design's `thing(...)` spelling cannot
be a function name: `thing` is a reserved struct keyword. `map_thing` performs
that operation without changing the language. `tools/testmap.braw` rebuilds
`data/test.map`; run `mdhavers run games/bogle/tools/replaybuild.braw` from the
repository root to rebuild the 350-tic input route. The route visits all eleven
convex sectors and crosses every portal, including all four stairs and the joins
generated from the L-shaped room.

BOGMAP 1 records follow design section 4.5. Texture ids 0..9 select granite,
flagstone moss, slate underside, dungeon brick, oak panel, thistle tapestry,
Celtic lintel, cell door, portcullis and peat straw. IDs 10..14 add bog, chapel
stone, fryer oil, exit lever and loch water. IDs 15..18 are granite with the cow portrait, stag head, banner, and the marked exit gate. Other accepted IDs use granite.
Ceiling texture -1 means sky. Sector kinds are normal 0, door 1, fryer 2,
bog 3, cold water 4, lift 5 and hidden door 6. A sector tag's low byte is the
door lock: 0/1/2 mean unlocked/iron/silver; `tag / 256` is a remote link tag. Thing type 1
is the player start; 2..5 select gamekeeper, haggis, tartan ghost and midges.
Types 10..20 are oatcake, haggis plate, juice can, juice bottle, shortbread, Mars
box, powder, blunderbuss, bagpipes, iron key and silver key. Type 21 is the exit;
30..36 are stag head, armour, brazier, thistle, cow portrait, banner and curling
stone. Exit thing flag bit 2 hides its sprite when a painted gate supplies the picture; the use trigger remains. Wall flag bit 1 blocks.
Wall flag bit 2 makes a solid wall a switch for link tag `flags / 256`; bit 4
makes it repeatable. Texture ids 19 and 20 are the switch's off and on faces. Doors move their ceilings and wait for bodies
to clear before closing. Loader
validation pairs exact geometric reversed edges, rejects partial or repeated
edges, enforces convex CCW polygons and reachability, and locates the player.

`tools/png.braw` decodes eight-bit gray, RGB, indexed and RGBA PNGs. It implements
stored, fixed and dynamic deflate, checks Adler-32, reverses all five filters,
and rejects interlacing. PNG chunk CRC is outside the selected subset. Fixture
creation and exact output are documented in `tests/png/README.md`.

`tools/bake.braw` is a library; `bake_all.braw` writes the asset manifest and the
colormap. It box-filters textures to 64x64 with periodic sampling, fits cropped
sprite content in 64x96 with visible feet on row 95, fits weapon frames in
128x96, and resizes sky to 256x128.
Alpha below 128 or RGB within 24 per channel of magenta masks a source pixel.
Sprites, weapons and HUD faces additionally mask magenta dominance (red and blue at least
128 and each at least 64 above green), removing antialiased purple fringes.
Textures and sky retain their original predicate so natural purple stays opaque.
A filtered texel is opaque at 50% foreground coverage; foreground RGB alone is
averaged and quantised to the nearest palette index 0..254 (lowest index wins ties).
BOG files have `BOG1 width height\n` headers and column-major payloads. Colormap
columns are levels (`BOG1 32 256\n`), indexed `level*256+colour`; each level scales
RGB by `(32-level)/32` before nearest-palette lookup. Transparent 255 stays 255.
Real granite and gamekeeper fixtures rebake and compare complete committed bytes.
Run the baker natively for bulk art; interpreter rebake fixtures use the harness's
existing 300-second per-process ceiling on a loaded shared box.

Sound is synthesized and mixed in `audio/mixer.braw`: mono signed 16-bit
little-endian PCM at 22050 Hz, with twelve bounded voices, integer envelopes,
seeded noise and clipping. Slot zero sustains the bagpipe drone while filling;
release gives a blast or a sad deflate. The shell reconciles the drone with held fire, the selected pipes, active charge and live play state each frame; it fades away when that cause ends. Other procedural cues cover the sgian-dubh
swish, Mars throw/splat, blunderbuss, door grind, pickup chime, gamekeeper shout,
haggis fuse/explosion, ghost wail, midge buzz, player oof and menu blips. The
one baked effect is the gamekeeper's first-sight roar: `tools/bake_roar.braw`
synthesises `data/sfx_keeper_roar.bmu` offline (an integer source-filter voice:
jittered, growling glottal pulses falling from about 175 to 100 Hz through four
"r"-to-"aa" formant resonators, with breath noise). Run it from the repo root to
rebake; output is byte-identical each run. It is BMU1 with loop start N (no
loop). The shell loads it at startup like the music and the mixer plays it once
on the `keeper_roar` event, emitted alongside the usual grunt only on the tic a
keeper first sees you; later windups only grunt. A missing or invalid file
means no roar. `tests/keeper_roar.braw` covers the event and playback. There
are no other effect sample files or new dependencies. Optional music is streamed from indexed mu-law files described below. Effects are mono, without
positional panning or distance attenuation.

`tic` returns a fresh list of sound event names; the pure front end collects
all due tics' events for its host. These outputs do not affect simulation or
rendering. The native shell fills to 1323 queued samples (60 ms) each frame;
three approximately 5 ms AudioQueue buffers account for the device's extra
buffering. Pausing fades the drone; resuming only sustains it while fire is held and the pipes are actively filling. A new run resets effect voices while preserving music and its on/off setting. The existing headless and replay hosts import no
audio bridge; audio does not affect their frame hashes.

The OS-only native bridge provides `sound_open(rate, channels)`,
`sound_queue(bytes)`, `sound_queued()` and `sound_close()`. It accepts 22050 or
44100 Hz and one or two channels. Queue input must contain complete signed16
little-endian interleaved frames; the bridge copies borrowed bytes before
returning. `sound_queued()` counts **scalar samples**, including samples already
in device buffers: stereo frames count twice. The capacity is 1 MiB (524288
scalar samples). Close is idempotent and clears the queue; queued returns zero
when closed. Device calls must run on the main thread; AudioQueue's callback
only copies the locked C ring and never enters mdhavers. Only programs using
the sound names link AudioToolbox.

`MDH_SOUND=none` or `MDH_SCREEN=none` selects a deterministic null bridge that
counts samples without draining them or opening a device. The game shell skips
audio initialization under either flag. On other native platforms and in the
interpreter, the API currently uses null sound; interpreter state belongs to
each interpreter instance. Native macOS reports an error if a real output
device cannot open. Arthur's listening check needs a logged-in session with an
available Core Audio output device.

`tests/audio.braw` hashes every timbre and checks sustain/stop, chunk independence,
finite tails, voice bounds and clipping. `tests/sound_events.braw` checks engine
and menu events. The Rust bridge gate also exercises lifecycle, input validation,
null backends, thread restrictions, framework isolation, and a device-free C
callback harness for borrowed copies, ring wrapping, in-flight counts and
underrun silence.

Music files are optional `data/music_title.bmu` and `data/music_gatehouse.bmu`.
The shell loads them once; a missing, malformed or unsupported file means
silence. BMU1 is a 16-byte header: ASCII BMU1, then big-endian u32 sample rate
22050, sample count N, and loop start L (0 <= L < N), followed by exactly N
8-bit G.711 mu-law mono samples. The mixer decodes through a 256-entry integer
table, plays the intro once, then samples L..N continuously. Music uses 48/256
gain below effects, with 512-sample fades/crossfades (about 23 ms). Play selects
gatehouse; all other screens select title. Pause offers Music ON/OFF; M is the
shortcut. Muting fades while the track position keeps moving. Restart keeps
the setting. Track composition and `tools/bake_music.braw` belong to the music
lane, separately from playback.

## E1M2: The Distillery

`maps/e1m2.braw` builds `data/e1m2.map` (`mdhavers run games/bogle/maps/e1m2.braw`):
155 convex sectors (E1M1 has 43) and 55 things. The builder collects axis-aligned
rectangles first and inserts every other rectangle corner that lies on an edge, so
cells of different sizes share exact portal edges. It also rejects a sector with two
portal walls on one line: the engine's crossing test uses each wall's whole line, so
such a sector would send the player into the wrong neighbour.

The start is a bright loch-side yard under open sky, with cold shallows, midges and
a boathouse alcove behind a secret door in the east wall. Its gate opens on the mash-tun hall hub, a ring of cells round a
solid oak tun with a raised balcony to the north. Three wings branch from the hub:

- **West (open):** a stair down to the dim, sunken malting floor and kiln. The airn
  key sits on the kiln gallery, a ledge seen from below and reached by a lift from
  the kiln column. The peat store is an optional alcove.
- **East (airn door):** a stair up to the dark bonded warehouse, where cask stacks
  form aisles. The blunderbuss lies under the lamp by the entrance, and the cooperage
  is an optional alcove behind a secret panel. Walking into the middle aisle
  opens a closet in the cask stack beside it and lets a keeper out. The siller
  key is on the lit plinth of the tasting room, with keepers waiting in the dark
  bays (the ambush). A switch on the loading dock's south wall raises the shutter
  onto the hall: the airn shortcut.
- **North (siller door):** the wash back and the still house, with two copper stills,
  hot wash floors between them and the bagpipes on the worm-tub platform. A stair
  climbs to the spirit safe and the exit chamber. The painted gate's use trigger is
  8 units in front of the gate face. The high walkway leads back to the hall balcony
  (juice bottle and shortbread), which drops into the hub: the siller shortcut.

It follows E1M1 in a new game, or play it directly with `BOGLE_MAP=games/bogle/data/e1m2.map`. `replays/e1m2.txt` is a
5295-tic completion generated by `tools/route_e1m2.braw`, an autopilot that drives `tic()`,
follows waypoints, opens doors and both secrets, presses the dock switch, rides the
gallery lift and fights whatever it sees, including the closet keeper. After a gameplay change,
regenerate it natively (`mdhavers build -O0 games/bogle/tools/route_e1m2.braw -o .scratch/route`,
then `BOGLE_ROUTE_OUT=games/bogle/replays/e1m2.txt .scratch/route`; `BOGLE_MAP` selects the
map). It prints milestones, the secrets, switches, closets and lift rides used, and the
lowest health; it fails on death, a stuck leg or an unused mechanic. Check it with the native headless build and `BOGLE_MAP=games/bogle/data/e1m2.map
BOGLE_REPLAY=games/bogle/replays/e1m2.txt BOGLE_W=80 BOGLE_H=50 BOGLE_EVERY=140
BOGLE_REQUIRE_EXIT=1` under `MDH_RC=0` and `MDH_RC=1`; both print `replays/e1m2.hashes`.
The interpreter's first 140 tics print its first line. `tests/e1m2.braw` asserts the
structural facts, and `tools/e1m2_shots.braw` writes 320x200 review PPMs to
`.scratch/e1m2shots/`. The engine's fixed strings still name the Gatehouse
(the level-complete message and the key pickup messages).

## Lifts, switches, secret doors and closets

These Doom-style mechanics live in `engine/doors.braw` beside the doors, use the
same 64-unit use trace, and add nothing to old maps (`tests/mechanics.braw`).

- **Lift (kind 5).** It rests at its authored floor. E on its face from below,
  or a switch with its link tag, lowers it 4 units a tic to its lowest
  neighbour's floor. It waits 105 tics, then rises 4 units a tic. Whoever
  stands on it rides it both ways: their feet follow the floor every tic. It
  will not rise onto a walker whose centre is off it but whose body overlaps
  its edge; that holds its timer, and a rising lift drops again. The prompt
  is **E: lift**.
- **Switch (wall flag 2).** E on the switch wall opens every door and lowers
  every lift whose link tag matches, plays the door sound and swaps the face
  to texture 20. One-shot switches stay pressed; repeatable ones (flag 4)
  pop back after 35 tics. Doors opened this way stay open. A door with a link
  tag ignores E ("It's shut fast. There must be a switch."), with no prompt.
  The prompt is **E: switch**. `tools/bake_decals.braw` bakes the two faces.
- **Secret door (kind 6, link 0).** A door that keeps the surrounding wall
  texture: it gets no gate face and shows no prompt. The first opening
  shows "Ye found a secret!" and counts `g["secrets"]` against
  `g["secret_total"]`; after that it is an ordinary door.
- **Monster closet (kind 6 with a link tag).** A hidden door opened, once,
  when the player's centre enters any other sector carrying that link tag (a
  walk-over trigger). It does not count as a secret and shows no message.
  Keepers behind it act on sight, which the closed door blocks, so they
  stay in the closet until it opens.

## Level progression

`shell/frontend.braw:bogle_levels` lists the episode in order. Winning a
listed level goes straight to the next level's briefing, carrying health,
spirit, ammo, owned weapons and the selected weapon; keys stay with their
level. Restart replays the current level with what was carried into it, New
Game starts again from the first level, and winning the last level (or a
map launched outside the list) shows the completion screen.
